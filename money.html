How this is calculated

Sell price. The lower of the cheapest listing on your faction's AH and the average price units actually sold at (from the hourly sales tracker), minus the 5% AH cut. Prices move while you sell: when an item's price is falling, it is projected one day ahead. The bot tracks a market price (the average of the cheapest 10% of listed units, so one cheap listing can't fake a crash); until it has 24 h of that history the trend comes from sold prices, counted at half. A rising price is never projected. If that is below the vendor sell price, the item is valued at vendor price instead and flagged vendor it. You sell on your own faction's AH; Blackwater is only used to buy reagents.

Deposit. Assumed to be 15% of the item's vendor sell price per listing (the Classic faction-AH rate for a 12-hour auction). It is returned when the item sells, so only expired listings cost it: we charge one lost deposit per unit sold, two when the market is oversupplied. Items with no vendor price have no deposit.

Reagent cost. For each reagent the cheapest of: the vendor, the AH, or crafting it yourself from a recipe you know (bolts, bars, leather, up to 2 levels deep; tick Mining to smelt). AH reagents are priced by walking the order book for a whole day's worth of crafts, not just the top listing, so a single cheap listing can't create a fake margin. When the AH can't supply a full day, your crafts per day are capped at what it can supply.

Own gathered reagents. If you tick a category under I gather my own, those reagents cost what you would get by selling them on the AH instead (cheapest listing − 5%). That is the honest cost: farming isn't free, and crafting only pays if it beats selling the raw materials. You can switch this to nothing to see the profit including your farming time.

Faction patterns. A pattern sold by a vendor or given by a quest only for the other faction is counted only when someone has listed it on your AH (at that price); otherwise the craft is hidden.

Profit per craft = sell price × units made − reagent cost − your time value per craft. Margin = profit ÷ reagent cost.

Sales per day come from listings that vanished between hourly snapshots before they could expire (cancelled listings look the same, so this can overstate demand a little). Days of supply = units listed ÷ units sold per day. With more than 2 days of supply on the AH your share shrinks in proportion (others are ahead of you in the queue); above 3 days the market is flagged oversupplied.

You sell per day = sales per day × your share. Profit per day = profit per craft × crafts you can sell per day. This is what the list is ranked by: a big margin on something that sells once a week is worth less than a small one that sells fifty times a day.

Before 6 hours of sales history there is no measured rate yet, so demand is guessed as a quarter of the units listed now, per day. Those rows are marked guess.

Reliability flags

few sales (fewer units sold than your minimum), one listing (the price rests on one listing and nothing sold) and price spike (the recent price is over 1.5× the earlier average, which can mean manipulation) are hidden unless you tick Show unreliable markets. thin reagents (a day's reagents cost 15%+ more than the top listings), oversupplied, reagent supply, price falling (down 5%+ a day) and supply rising (50%+ more listed than 24 h ago: others are making it too) are shown as warnings. Arrows next to a price show its trend where there is history.

Patterns, stations and what's left out

Pattern recipes show the pattern's cheapest AH or vendor price and how many crafts it takes to pay it back; patterns from Favor vendors or drops have no price. Recipes that need a rare station are hidden unless you tick that option.

Cooldown crafts (transmutes, Mooncloth and similar) are left out of the recipe data. Enchantments go on gear and can't be sold, so only enchanting items (rods, oils, wands) are listed. Items that were never listed or sold have no price; they may be bind-on-pickup (the data has no binding info) or just not traded yet. Each craft is ranked on its own: two crafts that use the same reagent compete for it.
