How the route is chosen

Each skill point uses the recipe with the lowest expected cost per point right now. Orange always gives a point; yellow and green give one with chance (grey − skill) ÷ (grey − yellow). Each step's details show its thresholds as yellow green grey.

Open a step to see its alternative: the next-cheapest recipe at that point, how much more (or less) it would cost over the same skill points, and a "Use it instead" button that drops the current recipe and re-plans.

Three rules keep the route practical, like a written guide. It stays on the current recipe unless another one is more than 10% cheaper, so it doesn't switch back and forth for a few copper. With "Avoid recipes needing over 3 crafts per point" ticked, it skips slow green recipes whenever a faster one exists. And every craft costs the time value you set (5 silver by default), so it prefers fewer crafts.

What you make is kept for later steps when a later recipe needs it (blasting powder for bombs, bolts for tailoring, grinding stones for weapons), like the written guides do: those steps say "keep N for later" and "uses N from earlier steps", and the route plans twice so it knows which reagents are worth making early for skill points. Tools are bought or crafted once, the first time a step needs them: Blacksmith Hammer from a vendor, Arclight Spanner and Gyromatic Micro-Adjustor crafted, and the runed rods every enchant needs.

Patterns: "Trainer + patterns on AH / vendor / quest" uses patterns you can actually get (vendor prices and NPCs are Classic data; quest patterns such as the Imperial Plate plans count as free). "All patterns" assumes you have every pattern, including drops and new Forever patterns whose source we don't know yet.

The route stops at each rank cap (75, 150, 225) and shows where to train the next rank. Journeyman needs character level 10, Expert level 20, Artisan level 35, so a lower "Character level" ends the route at the last rank you can train.

Reagents come from vendors when cheaper, otherwise from the cheapest auction listings still left, or are crafted yourself (bolts, bars, leather…) when that is cheaper. Click ✕ on a step to drop a recipe you don't want; the route is re-planned without it.

"Copy for Auctionator" copies the shopping list's AH items as an Auctionator shopping list (exact item names with the quantity to buy) named after the profession and skill range: in game open Auctionator's Shopping tab, click Import and paste it. "Copy as text" gives plain lines such as "20x Linen Cloth", vendor items included.

Materials you already have (under More options) are used before anything is bought and count as free, since you own them already: steps say "uses N from your bags", the shopping list shows "have N" and buys only the rest. Names are matched to the game's item names, ignoring case; lines that match no item are listed under the box.

"Copy link to this plan" copies a link with your settings in it (profession, skills, selling mode, recipes, faction, character level, time value, excluded recipes, fishing settings and, when the list is short, your materials). Opening the link shows the same plan, priced with that moment's auction house; the page also keeps its address up to date as you change settings. Crafting stations aren't in the link.

Trainer fees are included: each trainer recipe's cost is paid once, on the step that first uses it ("incl. training"), and each rank's fee (Journeyman 5s, Expert 50s, Artisan 5g) on its training row. Forever's trainer prices and learn skills come from Wowhead's Forever data. Cooking and First Aid's higher ranks come from books or quests, not a trainer fee.

The route switches recipes only when the other one is clearly cheaper (15%, and at least a minimum per skill point that grows with level) and stays usable for at least 5 more points, so it doesn't jump between items to save a few copper.

Fishing

Pick Fishing or Fishing + Cooking as the profession. A cast catches something with chance (your skill − the spot's base skill + 5)%, where your skill includes the pole and lure; below the base skill nothing bites, and from base + 95 nothing gets away (25 in the starting zones, 75 in the capitals and level 10–20 zones, 150, 225, 300 and 425 further out). Fishing skill goes up on successful catches only, and where you fish doesn't change the rate: 1 catch per point up to 100, then one more catch per point every 25 skill (10 per point at 290–300), about 1,280 catches for 1–300.

Each skill point picks the spot and lure with the lowest cost: hours spent × what your time is worth, plus lures, minus what the catch sells for (on the AH up to your share of measured demand, the rest at the vendor). It only moves to another spot when that is clearly better, since travel isn't free, and only to zones your character level is suited for. Ranks: Journeyman at skill 50 from any fishing trainer (level 10), Expert with the book "Expert Fishing - The Bass and You" from Old Man Heming in Booty Bay (skill 125, level 20), Artisan from Nat Pagle's quest in Dustwallow Marsh (skill 225, level 35).

Fishing + Cooking plans both: the fishing route first, then the cooking route uses the fish you caught before buying any (each fish counted at what you'd have sold it for). With "Cook them first" ticked (the default) the cooking route treats your catch as nearly free (its vendor price), so it cooks the fish you caught before buying anything; the totals still count what you give up by not selling them raw. Untick it to cook a fish only when that beats selling it. The fishing route values fish the cooking route needs at what buying them would cost, so it prefers spots with the fish you'll cook. Cook a step once you've caught its fish.

Fishing data: Classic 1.12 server tables (zone skills and catch tables), whose skill thresholds match WoW Forever's; Forever's fishing camp objects (Fishing Rack, Fishing Hut) and new baits aren't included. Fish shares are averages: Nightfin Snapper bites at night and Sunscale Salmon by day, and Winter Squid (Oct–Mar) or Summer Bass (Apr–Sep) depend on the season. Schools (pools) aren't modelled.

How AH sales are estimated

Blizzard's API lists auctions but never says what sold. Every hour the price job compares the new snapshot with the previous one: a listing with 2+ hours left that is gone an hour later can't have expired, so it was bought or cancelled by its seller (the two look the same). That gives units taken off the AH per day for each item: an upper limit on real sales, used as demand.

The plan only puts on the AH what you could sell at half of that daily demand over the time you pick, priced at the lower of the cheapest listing now and the average price items actually sold at, minus the 5% cut. The rest is vendored. Prices move while everyone levels: when an item's price is falling, the plan assumes it keeps falling until the middle of your selling time (it never assumes a rise), and when more than 2 days of supply is already listed your share shrinks in proportion. The sell list shows each item's trend and warns about crowded markets; when the bot publishes new prices the page offers to re-plan. Deposits and undercutting wars are not included, so the AH total is still an estimate; the vendor total is guaranteed.

About the data

Recipes, reagents and skill-up colours come from the live WoW Forever client (via the Blizzard Game Data API and wago.tools client tables), so Forever's own changes are included — for example sharpening stones turn grey much earlier than in Classic. The client doesn't say at which skill a trainer teaches a recipe, so that comes from Classic, lowered by as much as Forever moved the recipe's colours (Forever teaches many Tailoring, Blacksmithing and Leatherworking recipes 20–30 points earlier). Vendor prices come from Classic data. Forever turned many recipes into patterns (many bought with Merchant's Favor from the faction camp vendors); pattern steps are tagged, with the auction house price when one is listed. Some recipes need crafting stations such as an Anvil, Forge, Tanning Rack or Sewing Machine.
