How the route is chosen

Each skill point uses the recipe with the lowest expected cost per point right now. Orange always gives a point; yellow and green give one with chance (grey − skill) ÷ (grey − yellow). Each step's details show its thresholds as yellow green grey.

Three rules keep the route practical, like a written guide. It stays on the current recipe unless another one is more than 10% cheaper, so it doesn't switch back and forth for a few copper. With "Avoid recipes needing over 3 crafts per point" ticked, it skips slow green recipes whenever a faster one exists. And every craft costs the time value you set (5 silver by default), so it prefers fewer crafts.

What you make is kept for later steps when a later recipe needs it (blasting powder for bombs, bolts for tailoring, grinding stones for weapons), like the written guides do: those steps say "keep N for later" and "uses N from earlier steps", and the route plans twice so it knows which reagents are worth making early for skill points. Tools are bought or crafted once, the first time a step needs them: Blacksmith Hammer from a vendor, Arclight Spanner and Gyromatic Micro-Adjustor crafted, and the runed rods every enchant needs.

Patterns: "Trainer + patterns on AH / vendor / quest" uses patterns you can actually get (vendor prices and NPCs are Classic data; quest patterns such as the Imperial Plate plans count as free). "All patterns" assumes you have every pattern, including drops and new Forever patterns whose source we don't know yet.

The route stops at each rank cap (75, 150, 225) and shows where to train the next rank. Journeyman needs character level 10, Expert level 20, Artisan level 35, so a lower "Character level" ends the route at the last rank you can train.

Reagents come from vendors when cheaper, otherwise from the cheapest auction listings still left, or are crafted yourself (bolts, bars, leather…) when that is cheaper. Click ✕ on a step to drop a recipe you don't want; the route is re-planned without it.

Trainer fees are not included (recipes cost a few silver each; rank training is roughly 5s at 50, 50s at 125 and 5g at 200).

How AH sales are estimated

Blizzard's API lists auctions but never says what sold. Every hour the price job compares the new snapshot with the previous one: a listing with 2+ hours left that is gone an hour later can't have expired, so it was bought (or cancelled by its seller, which this can't tell apart). That gives units sold per day for each item.

The plan only puts on the AH what you could sell at half of that daily demand over the time you pick, priced at the lower of the cheapest listing now and the average price items actually sold at, minus the 5% cut. The rest is vendored. Deposits and undercutting wars are not included, so the AH total is still an estimate; the vendor total is guaranteed.

About the data

Recipes, reagents and skill-up colours come from the live WoW Forever client (via the Blizzard Game Data API and wago.tools client tables), so Forever's own changes are included — for example sharpening stones turn grey much earlier than in Classic. The client doesn't say at which skill a trainer teaches a recipe, so that comes from Classic, lowered by as much as Forever moved the recipe's colours (Forever teaches many Tailoring, Blacksmithing and Leatherworking recipes 20–30 points earlier). Vendor prices come from Classic data. Forever turned many recipes into patterns (many bought with Merchant's Favor from the faction camp vendors); pattern steps are tagged, with the auction house price when one is listed. Some recipes need crafting stations such as an Anvil, Forge, Tanning Rack or Sewing Machine.
